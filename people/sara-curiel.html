Sara Curiel Manzanas

FAIR Data Steward

Email: sara [dot] curiel [dot] manzanas [at] upm [dot] es

Short Bio

Sara currently works as a Data Steward at the European Rare Disease Research Alliance (ERDERA), where she applies her background in bioinformatics, computational biology and the FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable and Reusable) principles to support the onboarding of research projects and data resources. She helps researchers and data stewards with a particular focus on data and metadata management, semantic modelling, interoperability and responsible data stewardship. As part of the Wilkinson Lab, she contributes to the maintenance and further extension of CARE-SM, supporting its application to responsible data stewardship. She also serves as the WP3 Technical Contact for the OSTrails project, contributing to the technical coordination of activities related to Open Science research planning and tracking. In a previous technician position, she also contributed to the development of an ontology for dynamic, ontology-based databases as part of the data management activities at the Centre for Plant Biotechnology and Genomics (CBGP). Before joining the team, she worked as a bioinformatician in a hospital as part of the SPIDeRR project (Stratification of Patients Using Advanced Integrative Modelling of Data Routinely Acquired for Diagnosing Rheumatic Complaints), where she performed data analysis and statistical modelling and contributed to the development of machine learning models for predicting future appropriate referrals from primary care to rheumatology specialists. Her research interests include FAIR data, the Semantic Web, ontology and software engineering, and the application of semantic technologies to biomedical and research data. Furthermore, she believes in the power of science from a holistic perspective, and in the opportunity to bring together knowledge from different fields (biomedicine, computer science, agriculture, microbiology, languages, etc) in order to achieve better results. She received her Bachelor's Degree in Biotechnology, with a specialization in Plant Biotechnology, and her Master's Degree in Computational Biology from the Universidad Politécnica de Madrid (UPM). Her Bachelor's thesis focused on medical biotechnology, while her Master's thesis investigated parameters for evaluating protein structure predictions, with a particular focus on proteins involved in prion diseases.
